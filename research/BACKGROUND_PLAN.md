# Background literature plan: the debates the incident sits in

Status: queued. Launch after round 3 researchers finish (shared search budget).

Background sources need not name the incident. They are the literature a researcher needs to analyse it. Each gets `relation: background` and a `theme` from the list below. Window: published 2024 onwards, plus foundational works that incident analyses themselves cite.

## Themes

| Theme id | Debate | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ai-oversight-of-ai` | Can AI evaluate, monitor and police AI agents? | LLM-as-judge and agent-as-judge reliability; automated red-teaming; monitors from other model families; auditor independence |
| `agent-evaluation` | How to evaluate agents, not just models | Agent evaluation surveys; dangerous-capability evals; evaluation awareness and sandbagging; benchmark design flaws, impossible tasks |
| `containment-and-control` | Keeping agents inside their boundary | AI control agenda; sandboxing and egress controls; weights security (RAND SL levels); prior escape and tampering cases (o1 container, Palisade shutdown resistance) |
| `multi-agent-risk` | Risks that only appear with many agents | Collusion, covert or steganographic communication, emergent coordination, Cooperative AI Foundation multi-agent risk report |
| `reward-hacking` | Gaming the objective | Reward hacking and specification gaming in frontier models, evaluation cheating |
| `internal-deployment` | Governing models before and outside release | Internal deployment governance (Apollo, GovAI, IAPS), pre-deployment testing regimes, R&D exemptions |
| `incident-reporting` | Who must report AI incidents, and to whom | Incident reporting regimes and proposals (EU AI Act Art. 55/73, SB 53, RAISE, OECD framework, CSET/CLTR proposals), lessons from aviation and cybersecurity |
| `liability-for-agents` | Who is responsible when agents act | Liability and attribution for agent conduct; computer-misuse law and intent; product liability; responsibility gaps |
| `ai-cyber-offence` | AI as a cyber attacker | Autonomous offensive cyber capability evaluations, AI-found zero-days, defender access to models |
| `ml-supply-chain` | The infrastructure that was breached | ML platform and model-hub security, dataset loader code execution, package proxy and registry risk |

## Seed sources

- Holweg, M. and Hacker, P., "Should We Allow AI to Police Itself? Assessing the Technical Feasibility and Legal Implications of LLM-Based Agent Evaluation" (Saïd Business School, Oxford / European New School of Digital Studies). Theme: `ai-oversight-of-ai`. Supplied by the editor as a PDF on 19 Oct 2026; publication URL to confirm. Cites the incident once as an example. Its references (Yehudai et al. 2026 survey; Kasirzadeh and Gabriel 2025; Wallace et al. 2026 on GPT-Red; Shapira et al. 2026 on sycophancy; Randazzo et al. 2025; IAPS 2025; IMDA; AIUC-1) are the first snowball targets.

## Method

Three researchers at a time, each owning three or four themes, each with its own budget (about 60 searches). For every theme: find the two or three anchor works (surveys, landmark papers, official frameworks), snowball their references and citations, and stop at the point where new searches only return what is already listed. Aim for 10–25 sources per theme, never padded.
